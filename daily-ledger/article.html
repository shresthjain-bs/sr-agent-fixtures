City approves harbour redevelopment after a decade of debate

By Amara Osei, City Correspondent · 8 min read · Published today, 07:00

The council voted seven to two on Tuesday to approve a sweeping redevelopment of the eastern harbour, ending ten years of stalled proposals, legal challenges and public consultation.

Supporters say the plan will bring 4,000 homes, a new transit link and a waterfront park, while creating an estimated 2,500 jobs during construction. Critics warn it could push up rents in the surrounding neighbourhoods and displace long-standing businesses.

What was approved

The approved masterplan covers 18 hectares of former dockland. The first phase, focused on public infrastructure and the transit link, is expected to begin next spring and complete in 2029. Residential construction would follow in three further phases through 2034.

Thirty per cent of the new homes will be designated as affordable housing, a figure that rose from an initial 20 per cent after months of negotiation with community groups.

What happens next

Detailed planning applications for the first phase are due to be submitted before the end of the year. The council has committed to a rolling programme of public consultations for each phase.

“This is a defining decision for the next decade of the city,” the council leader said after the vote. “We have listened, we have compromised, and we are ready to build.”
